Album Deep Dive

#1: Around The Fur - Deftones ★★★★★★★★☆☆

This album marked my introduction to nu-metal and alternative metal. I remember hearing mascara for the first time in elementary school and it totally left a stain on a corner of my music taste, evolving all the way to one of my other favorite bands; mannequin pussy. The dreamy yet heavy production, combined with Chino Moreno's awesome and gritty vocals, creates this atmosphere that feels both aggressive and beautiful. It shaped how I understand musicality in punk-leaning genres.

#2: Swimming - Mac Miller ★★★★★★★★★☆

Swimming totally captures vulnerability and raw emotion in a way few albums do. I remember going through a strong identity crisis and inability to express myself in my middle school years. Hiding in he cloet, while also struggling to maintain confidence in my masculinity. Mac's deep and introspective lyricism mixed with jazzy and soulful production opened my eyes to hip-hop's softer, more emotional side, allowing me to feel more comfortable and masculine at the time. Genuinely loving something that society deemed masculine even if as "Small" as a music artist felt so securing to me. Mac Miller is just so incredibly charming and bless his little soul, I remember being so crushed when he passed. This album continues to resonate with me so so deeply. I remember in 8th grade hearing about Mac's passing and some asshole getting paint on my Mac Miller shirt that my older sister gave me the same week which ultimately felt like him dying again! But alas, everything is transformative I suppose.

#3: Pure Heroine - Lorde

Pure Heroine was groundbreaking for me as a young listener. Lorde's moody production, minimalist beats, and critiques of youth culture made me feel understood. Her departure from pop convention showed me the power of artistic vision and authenticity.

#4: Hit Me Hard and Soft - Billie Eilish

This album demonstrates the evolution of modern pop. Billie's intimate vocal style and Finneas's minimalist production create an unsettling beauty that commands attention. The album's exploration of pain and emotion in quietly powerful ways impacts me continuously.

#5: Ctrl - SZA

Ctrl is a masterclass in contemporary R&B and soul. SZA's vocal control, the album's cohesive production, and her honest exploration of relationships and self-discovery created a work that feels both timeless and urgently modern. The album's emotional depth is unmatched.

#6: Freetown Sound - Blood Orange

Blood Orange's experimental approach to electronic, R&B, and ambient sound influenced my understanding of genre-blending. The album is lush, introspective, and layered with meaning. It taught me to appreciate music that challenges expectations and embraces complexity.

#7: Labyrinth (The Original Soundtrack) - David Bowie

As Bowie himself said, "I don't know where I'm going from here, but I promise it won't be boring." This soundtrack blends art rock, new wave, and theatrical elements into something utterly unique. Its fearless experimentation shaped my appreciation for artists who refuse to be confined.

#8: Blonde - Frank Ocean

Blonde is a meditation on longing, loss, and the passage of time. Frank Ocean's innovative production, abstract lyrics, and emotional vulnerability create an otherworldly listening experience. Every replay reveals new layers of meaning and emotion.

#9: COPINGMECHANISM - Willow

Willow's fearless exploration of identity, trauma, and healing through experimental hip-hop and R&B is inspiring. The album's dark, introspective production matched with vulnerable lyricism shows an artist unafraid to be raw and honest about pain and growth.

#10: Honeymoon - Lana Del Rey

Honeymoon captures cinematic melancholy and romantic nostalgia like few albums can. Lana's production choices, orchestral arrangements, and dreamy vocals create an immersive world of vintage Americana and doomed romance. It's endlessly captivating and emotionally resonant.
